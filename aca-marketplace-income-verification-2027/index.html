What to do when the Marketplace asks for income documents

For a household with variable pay, a Marketplace notice can be hard to translate into a next step. Start with the exact request and the deadline on the letter.

By AllCalls EditorialEdition Nov 12, 2026

A worker whose hours change every week may have an eligibility notice open on a phone and a year of pay stubs stuffed into a drawer. The first useful thing an agent can do is help them find what the Marketplace is asking to verify.

Read the notice together

Start with the item that needs verification and the due date on the notice. If the issue is the enrollment calendar itself, see the 2027 Marketplace and Medicare deadlines. Ask the consumer to open the notice in their Marketplace account and read the relevant section. Use the approved channel if documents need to change hands. A text thread or a shared inbox can create more places for sensitive files to sit.

HealthCare.gov lists acceptable documents by verification issue. A recent tax return may be a reasonable starting point if expected income is stable. Recent pay stubs, a letter about a job change, or a self-employment ledger can be relevant when income has changed or is harder to predict. Match the document to the notice and confirm what the Marketplace will accept for that issue.

Keep the projection separate from the proof

The Marketplace application asks what the household expects to earn in the coverage year. HealthCare.gov explains how to build that estimate. A prior-year statement can support the conversation, but the estimate may need to account for a new contract, fewer shifts, or an expected break in work.

End with a plain recap. Name the document, where the consumer will upload it, and when they will check that the Marketplace received it. If you schedule a follow-up, record the date in your approved system. Keep the notice date visible in that handoff so a colleague can help without making the caller repeat the story.
