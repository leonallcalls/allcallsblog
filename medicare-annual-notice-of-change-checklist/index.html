The Medicare letter worth opening before October

How insurance agents can help callers read the Annual Notice of Change and turn it into a useful fall review.

By AllCalls EditorialEdition Sep 24, 2026

A plan letter can sit next to the grocery receipts for weeks. A caller may only open it after hearing a neighbor switched plans. The Annual Notice of Change gives that caller a concrete place to start.

Find the right document

Medicare says people enrolled in a Medicare plan receive an Annual Notice of Change each fall. It describes changes that take effect in January. Start by confirming the plan and year printed on the notice. If the caller can’t find it, help them identify the plan’s document page or where to request a copy.

Read it in the order the household uses care

Start with anything the caller knows they will use. That may be a recurring specialist appointment, a drug refill, or equipment they rely on. Then review changes in premiums or other costs that could affect regular care. The notice can flag what needs a closer look. The full plan documents and current provider or pharmacy information help answer those follow-up questions.

A workable call summary fits on one short note. Capture the change the caller cares about, what you have confirmed, and what you still need to check. For example, “The notice shows a pharmacy change. We’ll verify your current store and the mail-order option before our next call.”

When open enrollment begins, confirm the current year’s plan choices through the Medicare Plan Finder and revisit the caller’s priorities. Someone who is happy with a plan that still meets their needs may choose to stay with it.
