What an inbound insurance call really costs

A field guide to comparing lead sources by cost per issued policy, using agent time and a consistent reporting window.

By AllCalls EditorialEdition Jul 21, 2026

Two agents can pay the same amount for an inbound call and be buying very different outcomes. One finishes in ten minutes. The other needs three callbacks and a second review. A cost-per-call report shows the price of each conversation. To compare sources, follow those calls through to issued policies.

Use one definition across sources

Choose an intake window, such as the calls first received in July. Define which expenses count. For a direct comparison, you could track paid call charges less credits, plus agent labor for intake and follow-up. Divide that total by the number of attributable issued policies from that group of calls after they have had time to mature.

Suppose 40 calls cost $1,200 after credits and generated $300 in agent time. If five policies issued, the cost per issued policy was $300. This is a worked example. Its purpose is to show why excluding agent time or counting applications as policies can change the result.

Track where the work went

Use a few clear outcomes, such as connected, follow-up scheduled, applied, and issued. Add an outcome for calls outside your service area or line of business. Pair the spend metric with the time agents spent on cases that failed to progress. Then you can ask whether the issue is with the source, the way calls are routed, or the staff available when calls arrive.

Compare like for like

Keep the reporting window, product line, and attribution rules consistent. Give recent calls time to develop before comparing them with a much older group. A small sample can swing after one case issues, so keep counts next to the averages. That context matters when the next decision is whether to increase spend.
