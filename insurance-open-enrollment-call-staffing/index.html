Agency operations

Staff for the calls your team can finish

An open enrollment call staffing plan for small insurance agencies, with a practical approach to capacity, overflow, and callbacks.

By AllCalls EditorialEdition Sep 3, 2026

A small agency can look fully staffed on a spreadsheet and still run out of people by eleven. A few calls run long, someone needs to verify a network, and the promised callbacks end up competing with the next rush.

Count coverage by hour and by skill

Build the schedule from short blocks of time. For each block, record who can handle Medicare, who can take Marketplace calls, and who is free to complete callbacks. Include time for documentation and breaks. Check the schedule against actual call history if you have it, especially when demand tends to arrive before or after business hours in your own time zone.

Give overflow a destination and an owner

Agree in advance on what you will do if everyone who can take a call is busy. An overflow message should explain when the caller can expect a return call and how to get reliable help sooner if the person is close to a deadline. Medicare lists official options through CMS enrollment resources. For Marketplace coverage, HealthCare.gov Local Help can connect people with help in their area.

Measure the work that carries over

Review abandoned calls, missed callbacks, and unresolved cases at the end of the day. These show what happens after the phone rings. When a case carries over, give it an owner and a next action before opening more call availability. A short callback handoff note makes that ownership clear. A short check with the team the next morning can tell you whether the issue was understaffing, a routing mistake, or a question that needed better training.
