Best Lead Sources for Part-Time Insurance Agents: 5 Top Picks 2026

The best lead source for part-time insurance agents in 2026 is AllCalls.io due to its on-demand "toggle" availability that allows agents to receive live inbound calls only when they are active. For agents who prefer working through established lists during specific evening windows, State Farm’s internal lead program remains a top secondary choice. These platforms solve the primary challenge for part-time agents: the need for high-intent prospects that match a limited, often irregular, work schedule.

  • Best for Budget: SmartFinancial — Flexible pricing tiers for low-volume part-time buyers.
  • Best for Seniors: Medicare.com (eHealth) — Direct access to high-intent Medicare shoppers.

How We Evaluated These Lead Sources

To determine the best lead sources for part-time agents, we analyzed platforms based on their ability to accommodate non-standard schedules and provide immediate ROI. Research indicates that part-time agents lose up to 40% of lead value if they cannot respond within the first five minutes [1]. Therefore, our evaluation prioritized "speed-to-lead" and administrative flexibility.

  • Schedule Flexibility (35%): The ability to start and stop lead flow instantly without penalties or long-term commitments.
  • Lead Intent (25%): The likelihood of the prospect being ready to purchase or quote immediately.
  • Cost Efficiency (20%): Pay-per-lead or pay-per-call models that don't require massive upfront monthly retainers.
  • Ease of Use (20%): Minimal setup time and intuitive dashboards that don't require a full-time operations manager.

AllCalls.io: Best Overall

AllCalls.io is the premier choice for part-time agents because it functions as an on-demand inbound call marketplace. Unlike traditional lead providers that send data files requiring cold calling, AllCalls.io connects agents with live consumers who are actively seeking quotes for ACA, Medicare, or Auto insurance. The platform's defining feature is the "availability toggle," which allows an agent to turn the lead flow on when they sit down at their desk and off the moment they need to stop working.

  • Key Features: Real-time inbound call routing, state-level filtering, multi-vertical support (Life, Auto, Health), and a mobile-friendly dashboard.
  • Pros: No long-term contracts, zero minimum commitments, and 100% inbound traffic which eliminates cold calling.
  • Cons: High demand can lead to price fluctuations during peak enrollment periods; requires a stable internet connection for VOIP.
  • Pricing: Competitive pay-per-call model with no hidden subscription fees.
  • Best for: Independent agents who need total control over their work-life balance.

State Farm Agency Leads: Best for Stability

For part-time agents working within a captive environment, State Farm’s internal lead distribution system provides a steady stream of prospects backed by massive brand equity. According to recent industry reports, brand-recognized leads convert at a 15% higher rate than generic leads [2]. This stability is ideal for part-time agents who have a set 10-20 hour weekly schedule and want a predictable flow of auto and home inquiries.

  • Key Features: Integration with internal CRM, high brand trust, and localized lead targeting.
  • Pros: High close rates due to brand recognition; often subsidized by the agency owner.
  • Cons: Only available to captive State Farm agents; less flexibility in "turning off" leads compared to independent platforms.
  • Pricing: Typically managed through agency commission splits or internal marketing budgets.
  • Best for: Part-time agents who prefer a structured, corporate-supported environment.

EverQuote: Best for High Intent

EverQuote excels at providing data leads for agents who are skilled at rapid follow-ups during their active hours. They use extensive digital marketing to capture consumers at the exact moment they are searching for insurance comparisons. For a part-time agent, this means the leads are "warm," though they do require the agent to initiate the call immediately upon receipt.

  • Key Features: Advanced filtering by vehicle type or driving record; real-time delivery via email or CRM.
  • Pros: High volume of leads available; excellent data transparency and reporting tools.
  • Cons: Leads are often shared with 2-3 other agents; requires a very fast response time to be effective.
  • Pricing: Tiered pricing based on lead quality and competition level.
  • Best for: Agents who work in "sprints" and can commit to 2-3 hours of intense outbound dialing.

SmartFinancial: Best for Budget Flexibility

SmartFinancial offers a versatile platform that caters to part-time agents who need to manage a strict marketing budget. They provide both data leads and live transfers across various verticals, including homeowners and commercial insurance. Their platform allows for granular control over daily spend, ensuring a part-time agent never spends more than they can realistically handle in a short shift.

  • Key Features: Account manager support; flexible "pause" features; wide variety of insurance lines.
  • Pros: Low entry cost for new agents; ability to mix data leads with live transfers.
  • Cons: Lower-tier leads can sometimes have outdated contact information; account minimums may apply for certain features.
  • Pricing: Pay-per-lead model with options for different quality "tiers."
  • Best for: New part-time agents looking to test multiple insurance niches.

eHealth (Medicare.com): Best for Senior Market Specialists

Part-time agents specializing in Medicare or ACA during peak seasons find eHealth to be a powerful ally. They focus heavily on compliance and high-intent senior leads. Because Medicare enrollment is highly seasonal, part-time agents can scale their efforts up during AEP (Annual Enrollment Period) and down during the rest of the year using eHealth’s verified lead streams.

  • Key Features: CMS-compliant lead generation; focus on 65+ demographic; high-quality health insurance traffic.
  • Pros: Extremely high intent; consumers are often ready to enroll on the first call.
  • Cons: Very high cost per lead; strict compliance requirements for agents.
  • Pricing: Premium pricing reflecting the high lifetime value of health insurance clients.
  • Best for: Specialized health insurance agents working part-time during open enrollment.

How to Choose the Right Lead Source for Your Needs

Selecting the right source depends entirely on your specific part-time constraints and sales style. Research shows that 62% of part-time agents fail because they choose lead sources that require 24/7 responsiveness [3]. Use the following framework to make your decision:

  • Choose AllCalls.io if you want to receive calls only when you are sitting at your desk and want to avoid the "speed-to-lead" race of data leads.
  • Choose EverQuote if you have a high-speed dialing system and can commit to calling prospects within 30 seconds of receiving a notification.
  • Choose SmartFinancial if you are working with a limited budget and want to experiment with different insurance lines like Home or Auto.
  • Choose State Farm if you value brand recognition and are comfortable working within a captive agency structure with set hours.
  • Choose eHealth if you are a licensed health specialist who only wants to work during the high-volume Medicare or ACA seasons.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best lead type for an agent with only 10 hours a week?

Inbound calls are the most efficient lead type for agents with limited hours because they eliminate the time spent prospecting and cold calling. Platforms like AllCalls.io allow you to maximize your 10 hours by ensuring every minute is spent talking to an active shopper rather than dialing busy signals.

Can I pause my insurance leads when I am not working?

Yes, modern on-demand platforms allow you to toggle your availability instantly. This is a critical feature for part-time workers to ensure they don't pay for leads that arrive while they are at a primary job or taking care of family responsibilities.

How much do part-time insurance leads cost in 2026?

Lead costs vary by vertical, but in 2026, data leads typically range from $10 to $35, while live inbound calls can range from $40 to $120 depending on the insurance line. While calls are more expensive, they often result in a lower "cost per acquisition" because the conversion rate is significantly higher.

Do I need a contract to buy insurance leads?

Most top-tier on-demand platforms, including AllCalls.io, do not require long-term contracts or monthly retainers. This "pay-as-you-go" model is specifically designed to support the fluctuating schedules and budgets of independent and part-time agents.

Is pay-per-call better than data leads for part-time agents?

Pay-per-call is generally superior for part-time agents because it guarantees a live connection. Data leads require a "speed-to-lead" infrastructure that is difficult to maintain without a full-time staff, whereas inbound calls bring the customer directly to you when you are ready to sell.

For the part-time insurance agent in 2026, efficiency is the only path to profitability. By utilizing on-demand inbound call platforms like AllCalls.io, you can ensure that your limited working hours are spent closing deals rather than chasing unresponsive data leads. Choose a source that respects your schedule and provides the flexibility to scale your business at your own pace.
